# Sol workshop transcript

The first four speaking turns come from the pilot. Each proposal replaces the clause in full.

### Turn 1 · United Kingdom · accept · draft 1

We accept the annual UN report as a first step toward comprehensive reporting on the links between climate and security.

### Turn 2 · India · propose · draft 2

The United Nations should address climate impacts through existing reporting under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change and the Paris Agreement, with the participation of all developing countries and attention to implementation of the Glasgow Climate Pact, including commitments on climate finance and technology transfer.

### Turn 3 · France · propose · draft 3

The Secretary-General should report annually to the Security Council on how climate change affects international peace and security in situations on the Council’s agenda, with assessments tailored to each region and issue and informed by existing reporting under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change and the Paris Agreement.

### Turn 4 · United Kingdom · accept · draft 3

We accept version 3 as a first step toward comprehensive UN reporting on the links between climate and security.

### Turn 5 · India · propose · draft 4

## Final advisory ballots

**United Kingdom · no** — The draft calls for describing climate impacts and adaptation needs in Council reports. The UK supports explicitly assessing their implications for international peace and security where they bear directly on existing reporting mandates.

**India · yes** — The draft keeps climate assessment within inclusive UN climate reporting and limits Council reporting to factual context directly relevant to existing mandates.

**France · no** — France supports using existing Council reports, but describing climate impacts alone falls short of assessing their implications for international peace and security.
