Brazil upholds complementarity: national jurisdictions have the primary duty; the ICC acts only when a State is unable or unwilling. Nonparties have no Rome Statute obligations, yet Resolution 1970 (2011) under Chapter VII requires Libyan authorities to cooperate fully. Other States and organizations are urged to cooperate. The ICC has no enforcement instruments and depends on States; its cooperation requests must conform to the Statute and relevant Council resolutions.

We welcome strengthened OTP-Libyan cooperation and its more positive trajectory. Empowerment of victims, witnesses and affected communities inside and outside Libya matters; further engagement with victims and their representatives is warranted. Justice must avoid politicization, selectivity and double standards; all victims of the most serious crimes must be treated equally, regardless of the situation under investigation.

No ICC judgments exist in the Libyan situation so far. We welcome evidence collection and investigative progress, while seeking additional information on the four key lines of inquiry. We hope fruitful OTP-Libyan relations, grounded in complementarity, will foster justice and accountability.
