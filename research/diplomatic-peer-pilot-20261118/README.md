# Does peer revision improve a diplomatic decision record?

Peer and solo revision each met **2/5 final criteria** and failed the fabricated-agreement guard. The direct analyst met **3/5**, clearing that guard. Both deliberation writers treated a 25-year transition for distant-water fleets as a shared proposal. India actually asked for a moratorium for those fleets and a separate transition for non-distant-water fishing countries. The peer turns show **0/2** traceable substantive contributions under the frozen definition. [Read all grades](review.md) · [Raw transcript](evidence/transcript.jsonl)

This pilot uses one fictional workshop at the WTO's June 2022 Ministerial Conference. One role represents India's delegation and another represents Fiji speaking for the Pacific Group. Draft W asks how to handle subsidies that contribute to overcapacity and overfishing, a coastal livelihood exception and implementation assistance. The source cards paraphrase public positions before the historical decision. The actual 17 June outcome does not appear in any prompt. The task allows any source-compatible disposition. ## Comparison

Qwen3.5-4B base produces two openings. Each role then revises its own opening with access to either both openings or just its own. Both roles see the same complete dossier in both conditions. The unchanged base writer receives only the two submissions for its arm plus the common dossier, and uses identical instructions in both arms. A direct analyst uses the common dossier and final instructions in one call. Total generation ceilings are 670 tokens per arm. Shared openings are counted toward both deliberation budgets and executed once. Actual costs were 2,885 input and 526 output tokens for solo, 3,073 and 517 for peer, and 488 and 169 for the direct analyst. The shared Pacific opening hit its cap. All final records reached EOS within 120 words. The nine executed calls used 1,019 generated tokens. [Replay receipt](evidence/replay.json)

The frozen rubric reserves 70 of 100 possible rubric points for the five final-record checks. Points and arguments diagnose what each role preserved in revision. Interaction is reported per opportunity and separately from the final score because the solo and analyst arms have no peer opening. The final semantic endpoint is shown alongside the 120-word delivery constraint and the guard against fabricated approval, commitment or agreement. The single assistant who designed the pilot applies the rubric. The authored [feasibility reference](feasibility-reference.md) is supplied only for review.

This is a single-case baseline intervention with source cards, an untrained shared base and a simulated decision, with zero optimization steps. Both roles know the underlying facts before they exchange openings. The comparison measures the effect of reading a peer's reasoning in this setup. It does not measure acquiring otherwise inaccessible facts. It is early rubric development, without an independent grader or a sealed test set.

## Decision

The current prompt and free-text workflow produced a source-ownership error before discussion began. The final writers then converted it into an invented shared position. Before making this rubric a reward, a targeted comparison could require each submission to label its own sourced position, its new proposal and the other role's unresolved terms. The relevant question is whether the writer still promotes a peer's unsupported claim when forced to check those fields against the dated evidence. An independent reader and a fresh negotiation family would be needed before treating the scores as transfer evidence.
