# Spend one question when two recipients can matter

Add a controlled counterfactual on the Iraq and Syria package menus.
Each role privately knows which packages it can accept. One question
can request one signed claim about one candidate, with independently
modeled delivery loss. In the central test both recipients can certify
an agreement if they also accept the other role's opening proposal.
The two chances of local compatibility and two delivery rates vary
independently. A second state tests certifying either agreement or
impasse from the final unresolved option, and a third already has a
certificate. These are fictional inventories, calibrated public
probabilities, and simulated delivery channels. Historical positions
do not change and textual agent errors are not modeled.

Learn the chair and the responding delegate together with a 96-step exact
expected team reward. The chair sees the public ledger and reliability
features, never the resolved private menus. The responder sees its own
compatibility for the requested option, never the other private card.
The signed claim is built by the simulator from that local state.
Reward a correct certificate, charge the same query and reply costs,
and preserve justified unresolved outcomes. Freeze the synthetic
training rates and test both UN menus at different rates. No examples
from the actual benchmark responses enter fitting.

Compare fixed first and second recipients, first possible proof,
delivery-first and compatibility-first policies, the strongest simple
planning control using both probabilities, and the exact public-belief
oracle. Report the learned actor swaps and absolute agreement and
impasse yield, including missed certificates and incorrect commitments.
Check both orders and a delivery-rate reversal. This is a structured
MARL diagnostic. It does not train a language model or modify v1.
