# Test recipient cross-play when two openers look identical locally

Freeze the existing recipient policies and verifier. Replace the
opener with two previously unused scripted partners. A high-grade
partner reports its highest available grade to the chair; a low-grade
partner publishes the lowest. Lexicographic package order breaks
ties. For the first comparison neither buys peer visibility. The
second comparison uses an identical paid convention when the local
highest grade is three. On those cards both partners choose the
highest grade and send it to the peer, while on all other cards their
different chair choices stay private.

Hold the world mixture balanced and give the recipient equal odds
over the two partner hypotheses. It sees its own card and incoming
grade or silence on the current turn. Compare calibration histories
containing just those local observations with histories that also
include the previous chair's authenticated first report or its
absence. The latter is a retrospective record that supplies no
extra current-turn evidence. Compute histories of 0, 4, 16 and 32
ordinary turns without collecting reward labels. Score the known
partner ceiling, the equal-mixture planner, current frozen fixed and
ranked recipients and posterior adaptation. Account for the usual
attempted-send fee. Historical receipt access is modeled as already
available, with no additional fee. Use the four reserved positive
grids and the canonical Mali and Myanmar-linked fixtures.

Evaluate each first-seat choice, grade-order response and delivery
against the existing deterministic final renderer. Independently
verify the payoff projection against the decision evaluator. This is
same-budget cross-play with authored partners, in a known finite
model. There is no new language generation or change to the closed
benchmark.
