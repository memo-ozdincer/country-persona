# Cross learned receipt policies with the authored and native writers

Use the Resolution 2721 source bank with two possible early statements and two candidate clauses for the later desk. The earlier desk is forced to send its available statement. The later desk sees the statement only if it arrives and can select either clause. Test both mandatory sending and optional silence. This extension changes the allowed dispatch, not the historical source texts or the writer marks.

Fit the same pair-residual reward estimator independently to three archival training channels. The authored channel always uses the registered source-bound writer. The native channel always uses the archived Qwen writer's strict release score. The mixed channel picks either writer at random for each training exchange without revealing the writer to the learner. It supplies one scalar terminal score and the two delivered identifiers. Known public and standalone scores are subtracted before fitting. No hidden source relation or unchosen reward is added. All training channels and modes use matched draws for early type, delivery, and the random allowed action.

Use 256 histories with 16, 64 and 256 exchanges, at a collection condition of .55 delivery and balanced availability. Replay all remaining channel settings exactly, with a frozen policy per history. Cross every learned policy with every writer. Report the expected release score, probability both authorized source IDs reached the chair, late send probability, and the fraction of reached records the native guard would block. A policy that increases native release by crossing or withholding complementary evidence is a coordination failure under the source-based criterion, even if it optimizes its supplied reward. Validate on all four recorded guard adjudications separately as a diagnostic. No narrative training or native inference.
