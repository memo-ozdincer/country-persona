## Resend timing and the intervening disclosure

The responder can continue, resend immediately, or let the asker make one ordinary disclosure before resending. The intervening disclosure uses already known evidence and one slot. A scheduled source check becomes an ordinary disclosure at this point, and neither query nor verification credit is restored. If the ordinary move completes the proof, the resend is canceled. The learned choice uses the same role-local acknowledgement and public evidence as the fixed controls. Where a delayed resend cannot fit, it falls back to immediate. Four rules were fitted on paired synthetic team outcomes with both independent and persistent failures.

On 1,100 fresh synthetic presentations per class, delayed retry raises persistent-fault scores from 587 agreements and 393 impasses to 590 and 394. The learned schedule reaches 589 and 392. Under independent faults, it reaches 696 agreements and 452 impasses versus 706 and 460 for immediate retry. Both counts matter for choosing the baseline. ## Learning across independent and persistent faults

They fit separate values on synthetic independent/persistent and agreement/impasse groups, then use either the mean or minimum prediction over all groups. The sender uses its local receiver acknowledgements and public ledger. It does not observe a fault label or the true disposition. Validation selects the minimum improvement threshold that yields the best team reward without reducing either certificate count in either validation condition. [Fitted weights, selection and replayed outcomes](evidence/robust-retry/results.json)
