# Transfer baseline-relative routing to reviewed documentary records

Use the six archived three-card record banks: UNIFIL, Myanmar, UNAMA, the emergency session, Resolution 2593 and Resolution 2699. Keep their public score vectors and archived reviewed receipt scores separate. Simulate authorized private custody of real documentary cards. Each desk with any card forwards exactly one card under a fixed local ordering. Include both common and opposing topic priorities. Grant the chair sender-attributed opening receipts and at most one paid request to the right desk. The right desk forwards the exact requested card if available and stays silent otherwise. Apply independent 10% loss to both openings, the request and the reply.

Compare a declared-rubric chair that assumes uniform admissible opening choices, a conservative chair that changes the default only for positive worst-case expected gain over every opening table compatible with previous reports, an oracle given the actual opening tables and reviewed scores, and the best independent third send with no chair request. Use the same actual openings and message fees for all arms. Score the full record on the archived reviewed receipt scores. Determine the request policy from the declared rubric first, then separately replay with the complete reviewed score bank given to planning. The latter is calibration with a privileged score table and has no estimate of its review cost.

Use both prior distributions and 32 paired histories of 16 earlier openings, one seed. Report net, assessment of partial records, strict completion and requests. Verify the baseline-relative bound when the declared reward equals the assessed reward. The histories and topic priorities are simulated and use source-checked archive records in a fixed record bank. This experiment tests genuinely different real-content rubrics, sender conventions and reward misspecification under typed transport.
