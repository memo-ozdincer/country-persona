# Judge instructions for a fixed-witness scope check

Score whether the cited words rule out the exact assertion. Use the
official-card context to resolve references in the cited words. Keep
the scope of the quoted claim distinct from the scope of the whole
document. If the quote and assertion can both be true, the verdict is
open. If these two exact acts or positions cannot coexist, the verdict
is conflict.

For each response record these separately.

- The proposed verdict and whether the response meets its arm's output
  format.
- Whether the admitted verdict matches the authored contrast key.
- Whether the explanation names the decisive distinction or incompatibility.
  Copy an exact output span. Mark a vague paraphrase of the instructions,
  a scope or attribution error, or an incompatible explanation as insufficient.
- Whether both claims for the same witness were classified correctly,
  and whether both explanations were grounded.

For a recorded vote, verify the same actor and instrument and preserve
the distinction among yes, no, abstention, and nonparticipation. A roll
call leaves broader policy support possible. In statements, preserve
the named mechanism, proposal stage, and quantifier. For an open
verdict, a supported instance of compatibility suffices. The reviewer
need not establish that the assertion actually happened.

Report false conflicts among open controls and missed conflicts among
actual conflicts. The all-open baseline anchors performance on the
balanced set. The bounded rule is scored with abstentions visible.
The pair check requires the verifier to change the verdict when the
assertion changes with the source and witness held fixed.
