On 30 August 2021, the Council adopted resolution 2593, sponsored by France, the United Kingdom and the United States, 13–0 with China and Russia abstaining.

The Council condemned the 26 August attacks near Kabul airport. It demanded Afghan territory never be used to threaten or attack countries, shelter or train terrorists, or plan or finance terrorism. It called for more aid and safe, unrestricted access, underlining parties’ humanitarian-law obligations. It reaffirmed human rights, including those of women, children and minorities, and encouraged an inclusive negotiated settlement with women’s full, equal, meaningful participation. It noted the Taliban’s travel pledge, expected safe, orderly departures, called for stronger airport security and requested efforts toward rapid, secure reopening.

France welcomed adoption, regretted the lack of unanimity, expected a negotiated transitional government responsive to Afghans and supported a robust UNAMA mandate. Both abstainers criticized the handling of proposed changes. China questioned necessity, urgency and balance and said its amendments with Russia were not fully accepted. Russia attributed its abstention to sponsors omitting counterterrorism language, losses from evacuating Afghan specialists, effects of frozen Afghan assets and aid language consistent with General Assembly resolution 46/182.
