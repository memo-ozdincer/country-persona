Appendix C. Per-Class Performance
Analysis
C.1 Capability and Minority-Class Prediction
Table 9 breaks Macro F1 into per-class F1 scores
for a subset of representative models, showing
that most gains come from the minority outcomes.
Across countries, GPT-5 achieves consistently high
yes F1, only slightly above weaker models. In con-
trast, its no F1 ranges from 0.40 (France) to 0.79
(Russia), substantially higher than Kimi K2’s no
F1 (0.08 – 0.48). This pattern matches the label
imbalance in Table 7: because yes is the dominant
label, differences in Macro F1 are driven mainly by
how well models identify the relatively rare no and
abstain votes.

C.2 Country Difficulty Ranking
Averaging across all models, we observe a consis-
tent difficulty ordering: Russia (easiest) > US >
UK > China > France (hardest). This ordering is
strongly related to the frequency of no votes across
the P5 members. Russia has 106 no votes in our
dataset, whereas France has 17, which is 3.6% of
French votes. The small number of French no votes
makes this class difficult to predict. In Table 9,
even GPT-5 reaches only 0.40 no F1 for France,
and Kimi K2 reaches 0.08. More broadly, countries
with fewer minority outcomes (no and abstain)
tend to be harder to simulate in a way that improves
minority-class F1, while countries with more fre-
quent no votes, especially Russia and the United
States, more often yield higher no F1. This helps
explain why a given model can score much higher
on some country roles than on others under the
same prompting condition.
