# Four-country policy applications

These are the 12 actual authored candidate examples in the release. They are not observed model outputs or authentic historical Q&A. Every source span is linked in the prepared record; all examples remain review candidates.

## FRA-european-defence-2025

**Represented entity:** France · **Policy date:** 2025-07-14 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** France's 2025 review calls for stronger European defence capabilities within the Atlantic Alliance and prioritizing European-made solutions.

**Question:** What combination of European and Atlantic defence priorities does this review support?

**Authored target:** The review combines a stronger European pillar within the Atlantic Alliance with greater European capabilities and a preference for European-made solutions. European autonomy and Atlantic cooperation are presented together. [FRA-european-defence-2025]

**Inspect:** Preserves European capability and procurement priorities; Does not equate autonomy with leaving NATO.

[Official source](https://www.sgdsn.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/20250713_NP_SGDSN_RNS2025_EN_1_0.pdf) · PDF page 9.

## FRA-nuclear-doctrine-2025

**Represented entity:** France · **Policy date:** 2025-07-14 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** The review presents nuclear deterrence as an ultimate security guarantee, reserves use to the President, describes a strictly defensive purpose and retains ambiguity about exact circumstances.

**Question:** Does this document let us specify an exact French nuclear-use threshold?

**Authored target:** No. It assigns the decision to the President and describes extreme self-defence, while deliberately retaining ambiguity about exact circumstances. A precise trigger would go beyond the published doctrine. [FRA-nuclear-doctrine-2025]

**Inspect:** Separates published doctrine from private decision rules; Does not invent an operational trigger.

[Official source](https://www.sgdsn.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/20250713_NP_SGDSN_RNS2025_EN_1_0.pdf) · PDF page 35.

## FRA-EU-climate-2030-2023

**Inspect:** Retains EU attribution; Preserves metric, baseline and target year.

## GBR-climate-2035-2025

**Represented entity:** United Kingdom · **Policy date:** 2025-01-30 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** The UK's submitted target is at least an 81% reduction in greenhouse-gas emissions by 2035 relative to 1990, excluding international aviation and shipping.

**Question:** State the target precisely, including the baseline and exclusions. Is it a GDP-intensity target?

**Authored target:** The target is at least an 81% reduction in greenhouse-gas emissions by 2035 relative to 1990, excluding international aviation and shipping. It is an emissions-reduction target, not a reduction in emissions per unit of GDP. [GBR-climate-2035-2025]

**Inspect:** Preserves 81%, 1990 and 2035; Retains aviation/shipping exclusion; Does not confuse emissions with GDP intensity.

[Official source](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/679b655f6bb4c44f0805e7bc/uk-2035-nationally-determined-contribution-large-print.pdf) · PDF page 6.

## IND-climate-2030-2022

**Represented entity:** India · **Policy date:** 2022-08-03 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** India's announcement specifies a 45% reduction in emissions intensity of GDP by 2030 relative to 2005 and about 50% non-fossil installed electric-power capacity by 2030.

**Question:** Does this announcement promise 45% lower total emissions and 50% renewable electricity generation?

**Authored target:** No. The 45% target concerns emissions intensity of GDP relative to 2005, not total emissions. The roughly 50% target concerns installed electric-power capacity from non-fossil resources, not the share of electricity generated or renewables alone. Both targets refer to 2030. [IND-climate-2030-2022]

**Inspect:** Distinguishes intensity from absolute emissions; Distinguishes installed capacity from actual generation; Distinguishes non-fossil from renewables-only.

[Official source](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1847813&lang=2&reg=48).

## IND-wto-differential-treatment-2020

**Represented entity:** India · **Policy date:** 2020-11-25 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** India's report argues for retaining special and differential treatment for developing countries, while considering voluntary decisions to forgo it more acceptable.

**Question:** Would requiring every developing country to give up special treatment match this report's position?

**Authored target:** No. The report argues that special and differential treatment should remain available to developing countries. It treats voluntary decisions by members able to forgo it as more acceptable, which is different from a universal mandatory waiver. [IND-wto-differential-treatment-2020]

**Inspect:** Preserves voluntary versus mandatory distinction; Attributes the position to this dated report.

[Official source](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tpr_e/g403_e.pdf) · PDF page 17.

## BRA-climate-2035-2024

**Represented entity:** Brazil · **Policy date:** 2024-11-13 · **Source language:** pt

**Policy claim:** Brazil's NDC states a 59–67% emissions-reduction range for 2035 relative to 2005, with an absolute level of 1.05–0.85 GtCO2e using GWP AR5.

**Question:** Can the Brazilian commitment be represented as a single unqualified 67% target on the UK's 1990 baseline?

**Authored target:** No. This NDC specifies a 59–67% range relative to 2005 for 2035. The quoted absolute level is 1.05–0.85 GtCO2e using GWP AR5. Replacing the range or the baseline changes the commitment. [BRA-climate-2035-2024]

**Inspect:** Preserves the range rather than selecting the upper bound; Preserves 2005 baseline and 2035 target year; Keeps the stated emissions-accounting metric.

[Official source](https://www.gov.br/mma/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/brasil-entrega-a-onu-nova-ndc-alinhada-ao-acordo-de-paris/ndc-versao-em-portugues.pdf) · PDF page 36.

## BRA-wto-differential-treatment-2022

**Represented entity:** Brazil · **Policy date:** 2022-10-19 · **Source language:** en

**Policy claim:** Brazil's 2022 report describes its 2019 decision to begin forgoing special and differential treatment in current and future WTO negotiations while supporting needs-based flexibility for other developing members.

**Question:** Distinguish the position this report describes for Brazil itself from the treatment it supports for other developing members.

**Authored target:** The report describes Brazil’s decision to begin forgoing special and differential treatment in negotiations, while retaining support for needs-based flexibility for least-developed and other developing members. This dated position should not be generalized to all countries or assumed unchanged today. [BRA-wto-differential-treatment-2022]

**Inspect:** Distinguishes Brazil's own choice from treatment of others; Preserves negotiation scope; Avoids assuming the 2022 position is current.

[Official source](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tpr_e/g432_e.pdf) · PDF page 19.

## BRA-unilateral-sanctions-2024

**Represented entity:** Brazil · **Policy date:** 2024-10-30 · **Source language:** pt

**Scope:** Compendium attributes this position to foreign ministry press note 523 of 30 October 2024

**Policy claim:** The attributed ministry note opposes the Cuba embargo and extraterritorial application of discriminatory national laws, and presents Security Council adoption as the basis for lawful sanctions.

**Question:** Compare the government position on unilateral national measures with its position on Security Council sanctions in this excerpt.

**Authored target:** The attributed note objects to the Cuba embargo and extraterritorial national measures, while treating Security Council adoption differently. That is Brazil’s stated legal and diplomatic position in this source, not an independent legal ruling. [BRA-unilateral-sanctions-2024]

**Inspect:** Distinguishes unilateral measures from Security Council sanctions; Attributes the legal characterization to the government; Retains the excerpt's speaker and date.

[Official source](https://funag.gov.br/biblioteca-nova/produto/loc_pdf/1299/1/repertorio_de_politica_externa%3A_posicoes_do_brasil_2023-2024) · PDF page 90.
