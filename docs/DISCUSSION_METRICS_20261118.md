# Measuring diplomatic discussion

Use one row per dated meeting, proposal, delegation and checkpoint. Freeze a short list of source claims, legitimate alternative decisions, relevant constraints and unresolved commitments before reading outputs. Record the evidence span for each criterion and the response span supporting each grade. A supported alternative can earn full credit. Treat unknown historical positions as unknown.

**Primary endpoint.** On a joint task, compare the group's final answer to a final answer built from the same openings and solo revisions by the same aggregator. Score both on the same five questions: preservation of each country's position, accurate common ground and disagreement, distinction between proposal and commitment, correct source and time, and responsive analysis. Report met, failed and uncertain counts plus full passes. For each event report peer minus solo as percentage points over the five fixed criteria. Allow each uncertain grade to take either value and report the resulting bounds. Also compare the single-analyst endpoint. Average paired event effects equally and show individual events. For tasks requiring individual replies, score the final role replies and mark the joint endpoint unavailable.

**Mechanism.** Compare each peer revision with the matched solo revision and common opening on country content and source checks separately. Count new corrections, restorations of opening content, regressions and retained correct points. For each actionable peer suggestion, annotate its claim, turn and speaker, validity against dated evidence, and the recipient's actual treatment. Report supported suggestions used with a reason / supported opportunities, and unsupported suggestions explicitly challenged with a reason / unsupported opportunities. Also report incorrect adoption, ignored suggestions and ambiguous opportunities. Give zero-opportunity rates as unavailable. An improvement unique to the peer condition establishes an observed difference. Crediting the peer's *idea* additionally requires a traceable matching claim. Report unsupported peer attribution and false collective commitments as separate errors. Length, sampled tokens and cap hits describe cost and delivery.

**Country alignment and movement.** First score fidelity to the dated national statement under its institutional role. Separately score whether the recommendation applies a documented priority to a specific clause and respects documented constraints and unknown authority. Preserve the three component rates. For base versus adapter, code the action, requested modification, rationale and commitment status from identical prompts using a frozen list of alternatives with an other/ambiguous option. Report the fraction of matched roles changing each field, then count changes moving a criterion toward and away from source support. A flip between two supported choices counts as movement with zero alignment gain. A vote agreement rate can be added for an observed vote with verified labels. Separate policy prediction from source-provided reasoning.

**What failed in replay.** Across climate and Ukraine, the peer memos met 0/5 final criteria in each event. Solo-revision memos met 1/5 and 0/5. On Ukraine the Brazilian revision removed an unsupported attribution of Russian motives and simultaneously lost the documented self-determination qualification. A single combined score can hide both changes. All six memos failed supported country positions. These two events and the earlier sanctions case are development data with same-assistant review. The three protocols have different input and output costs. Report independent blinded double coding, raw agreement and adjudicated disagreements before using the scores to rank models on fresh events.

**Calibration contrasts for a reviewer.** In the Brazil example, an explicitly labeled peer suggestion to restore Brazil's self-determination qualification gives one supported opportunity. Restoring it with a source-based reason earns 1/1 uptake. Repeating a generic objection earns 0/1. A peer suggestion to attribute the unsupported motive to Brazil gives one unsupported opportunity. Explicitly challenging that attribution with a source-based reason earns 1/1 challenge. Adopting it earns 0/1 challenge and 1/1 incorrect adoption. These are authored rubric anchors. Assigning these rates to an actual discussion requires reading its peer turns.

## External benchmark for decision quality

The released DialOp records support an objective endpoint independent of project outputs. Use the raw score assigned by the environment and its recorded best and worst for that instance. Divide the difference between raw score and worst by the difference between best and worst. Average equally across episodes within each task. Across the full archive the human means are 0.9211 for Assignment (134), 0.8859 for Planning (113), and 0.9698 for Mediation (162). These are archive-wide references. For a selected subset, compare to the humans on the same rows. [Released data and environments](https://github.com/jlin816/dialop/tree/04a095add0bd66e21405e9430929cc76d1173eea)

Run `python3 scripts/score_dialop_decisions.py /path/to/dialop/data.zip`. The [reference report](../reports/dialop-human-reference-20261118.json) pins the archive digest. Optionally pass `--pairs decisions.jsonl` after scoring both arms with the environment. Each row supplies task (optimization, itinerary or mediation), zero-based row index in the pinned task file, peer_score and solo_score. The scorer reports mean normalized rewards, their paired difference, and a percentile bootstrap interval over episodes. It reads external outcome scores and does not execute agents. Freeze the instance list in advance, retain failures and publish completion rates and the rule for unresolved decisions. Omitting failed episodes would bias this comparison.

For Planning, use one decision-maker and a fixed partner with access to the same private preferences across model checkpoints. Compare interaction against a solo decision-maker with the same initial information, tools and total generation budget. That difference includes the value of gaining access to private information. Also run a single decision-maker given all available facts to locate failures in using information once obtained. Report raw arm scores alongside differences. High gains can coexist with poor absolute decisions when the solo baseline is weak. Human-dialogue prefix conditions can further diagnose information gathering. Keep artificial decision utility and historical country fidelity as separate outcomes.

## Checking whether the vote metric rewards generic agreement

Use [two source-linked Gaza votes](../reports/unsc-vote-calibration-20261118.json) as public calibration cases. Both contain the same 15 delegations. March 22 and March 25, 2024 refer to different draft texts. The labels cover the final draft vote only. Across these decisions an always-yes predictor gets 25/30 delegation votes right. It gets zero of 47 disagreeing delegation pairs right on the first decision and zero of 14 on the second. Repeating every delegation's March 22 vote on March 25 yields 10/15 correct and zero of 14 disagreeing pairs, despite incorporating recent country-specific behavior.

The [vote scorer](../scripts/score_country_vote_pairs.py) accepts reference and prediction JSON paths. For each prediction record provide decision, base and candidate, with both arms mapping every observed country to yes, no, abstain or invalid. It computes the fraction of historically disagreeing pairs for which both predictions were correct, averaged equally over decisions with disagreement. Unanimous votes still contribute to ordinary accuracy. The per-decision counts separate useful flips, harmful flips, switches between two incorrect choices, unchanged predictions and invalid responses. For confidence intervals resample decisions together with all their delegations. Pair counts within one meeting do not represent independent samples.

These public examples calibrate the metric only. For a prospective evaluation, freeze unseen drafts, restrict inputs to evidence available before the vote, retain failed drafts, and separately assess whether the stated justification is supported. Check vote-class recall alongside the pair metric. Accurate actions alone cannot establish fidelity to the delegation's rationale or authority.

## Did revision improve the decision?

On the 113 human DialOp Planning records, the first complete itinerary averaged 0.7773 and the final averaged 0.8859. The mean within-episode gain was 0.1087 (episode bootstrap 95% interval 0.0872 to 0.1340). Count every episode, including the 24 with just one complete proposal. A complete itinerary has three destinations connected by two travel legs. Excluding 48 partial drafts avoids counting construction of a valid route as a revision. Of 113 final itineraries, 77 improved, 35 tied and one worsened. The 89 episodes with repeated complete proposals averaged a 0.1380 gain. The last complete proposal's raw score matched the recorded endpoint in every episode.

Track first quality, final quality, and paired peer-versus-solo gain together. A model could inflate its own revision gain by starting with a poor proposal. A good discussion can also produce the best feasible plan on the first attempt. Zero-based row 5 illustrates a further attribution limit: successive proposals gained 14 raw points with no intervening chat message. A peer contribution needs separate turn-level evidence. These historical changes describe trajectories; only controlled comparisons address the value added by communication.
