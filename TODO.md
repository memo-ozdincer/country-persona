**Rationale:** For diplomatic decision-making, a useful training unit is **dated information + concrete choice/proposal → observed action**, with public justification as a separate, attributed target. Decision cases are a high-priority acquisition hypothesis for this project, not a proven universally best training primitive. [UNBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14122) supports the relevance of linking proposals, votes and statements for diplomatic evaluation; it does not establish an optimal post-training curriculum.

**Causal limitation:** Observed actions, public explanations and subsequent outcomes do not by themselves identify causal drivers, private utility, negotiation thresholds or counterfactual decisions. Unrecorded information can confound policy learning and evaluation; see [Kausik et al., 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/kausik24a.html). Record a public justification as a claim, an analyst explanation as a hypothesis, and an observed outcome as an outcome—not automatically as a reward or causal effect. Counterfactual/causal learning would require additional assumptions and identification evidence or an independently validated intervention environment.

Keep policy documents, commitments, statistics and substantive Q&A as supporting evidence and supervision. The next step is to join existing evidence into better decisions, not discard the rest of the corpus or assume every training record must be a decision case.
