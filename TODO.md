# Data priorities

- [ ] **P0 — Expand linked decision cases beyond the two Ukraine resolutions.** Build a small, diverse pilot before increasing volume. Cover distinct events across several issue areas (security, climate, development/trade and human rights where comparable decisions exist), preserving agreement, disagreement and abstention. Sample events first rather than selecting only dramatic country differences.

  Each case should link the exact proposal and available alternatives/procedural stage; country and institutional role; information available before the decision; relevant dated commitments and material constraints where documented; the observed action; and separately dated public statements, requests/amendments and later outcomes. Preserve source spans, unavailable fields and original labels. A requested change is not automatically a necessary condition for agreement.

  Acceptance: expand beyond the current two-event coverage; report distinct event counts separately from country views; audit final versus procedural decisions, attribution, missing context and label linkage; retain whole event/translation families in a single split. Keep post-decision explanations and outcomes out of forecasting inputs. Existing exposed cases remain development material, not a fresh hidden benchmark.

  Evaluate the new batch first with the base model plus country prompting and with supplied evidence, then compare a small matched-budget supervised run. Inspect supported action choices, retained conditions, attribution, unsupported motives and supported agreement—not just different wording. Record whether decision-case supervision adds value over policy-application and authentic-Q&A supervision; the optimal mixture is not established.

**Rationale:** For diplomatic decision-making, a useful training unit is **dated information + concrete choice/proposal → observed action**, with public justification as a separate, attributed target. Decision cases are a high-priority acquisition hypothesis for this project, not a proven universally best training primitive. [UNBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14122) supports the relevance of linking proposals, votes and statements for diplomatic evaluation; it does not establish an optimal post-training curriculum.

**Causal limitation:** Observed actions, public explanations and subsequent outcomes do not by themselves identify causal drivers, private utility, negotiation thresholds or counterfactual decisions. Unrecorded information can confound policy learning and evaluation; see [Kausik et al., 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/kausik24a.html). Record a public justification as a claim, an analyst explanation as a hypothesis, and an observed outcome as an outcome—not automatically as a reward or causal effect. Counterfactual/causal learning would require additional assumptions and identification evidence or an independently validated intervention environment.

Keep policy documents, commitments, statistics and substantive Q&A as supporting evidence and supervision. The next step is to join existing evidence into better decisions, not discard the rest of the corpus or assume every training record must be a decision case.
